# Market structure guide

Read the chart in this order. Never call a direction before step 2
is done.

## 1. Find the levels

- **Support** — a price where the fall stopped and turned up, more
  than once. One touch is a guess; two or more touches is a level.
- **Resistance** — a price where the rise stopped and turned down,
  more than once. Same rule.
- **Zone, not a line** — a level is a band a few pips wide, not an
  exact price. A wick through the line that closes back inside the
  zone still counts as a hold. A close beyond the zone is a break.
- **Freshness matters** — a level tested five times is weaker than a
  level tested twice. Each test spends some of the orders sitting
  there. Prefer levels that held and have not been chewed through.
- **Higher-timeframe levels win** — a daily support matters more
  than a 15-minute support. Mark the daily and 4-hour levels first,
  then look at the lower timeframe inside them.

## 2. Read the structure

- **Uptrend** — higher highs and higher lows. The trade is to buy
  pullbacks to support, not to chase the high.
- **Downtrend** — lower highs and lower lows. The trade is to sell
  rallies to resistance, not to catch the falling knife.
- **Range** — price bouncing between a support and a resistance
  with no new high and no new low. The trade is at the edges, with
  a tight stop, and the middle of the range is no-trade land.
- **Breakout** — a close beyond a level, then a retest that holds
  as the other side. A break without a retest is a chase; wait for
  the retest or skip it.
- **No structure, no trade** — if you cannot say trend, range or
  breakout in one sentence, there is no setup. Wait.

## 3. Where is price now

- At support in an uptrend: the long side has the structure.
- At resistance in a downtrend: the short side has the structure.
- At support in a downtrend: do not buy the bounce unless the
  structure changes first — a bounce against the trend is a
  counter-trend trade and needs a smaller size and a tighter stop.
- Mid-range, mid-trend: nothing to do. The best trade is often no
  trade.
