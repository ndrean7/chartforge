## Description

Most losing trades are not bad analysis. They are trades taken
without a plan: no level, no stop placed where the idea is wrong,
no size decided before entry, and no written invalidation to tell
you when to walk away. ChartForge fixes the process, not the
prediction.

Give it a pair and a chart — or just the levels you see — and it
reads the market the way a signal provider reads it: where is
support, where is resistance, is this a trend or a range, and where
does the current price sit inside that structure. Then it checks
the setup against a pre-trade checklist — trend, level, confirmation,
stop placement, minimum 1.5R — and sizes the position from the risk
you chose, with a dependency-free calculator that loses exactly that
risk if the stop is hit. Finally it writes the whole thing down as a
plan in signal format: pair, direction, entry, stop, target, size,
and the invalidation that proves the idea wrong.

The method is the one behind a real copy-signal record on Traders
Family (profile AndreFx081, card oxndrean7): 33 months, +5,511.4
pips, 60K USD profit, about 2,898.5 pips per month on average, and
November 2026 at 3,360.3 pips across 41 signals. The skill also
teaches the habit that keeps a record honest: journaling every trade
and reviewing the month like a signal record — total pips, signals,
win rate, and whether the plan was actually followed.

What it does not do: it never promises profit, never presents a
signal as certain, and it is not financial advice. Past pips are a
record, not a guarantee — any month can be negative. It does not
read live prices or place trades; the buyer brings the chart, the
skill brings the structure, the checklist, the math and the journal.

## Example session

User: "SOL is at 145, support at 143 held twice, is this a long?"

The skill reads the structure: higher highs and higher lows, an
uptrend, price pulling back to a twice-held support — the long side
has the structure. It runs the checklist (trend, level, confirmation
close, stop below the zone at 140, target at the next resistance
162, 3.4R), sizes the position ($1,000 account, 1% risk = 2 units,
lose $10 if stopped out, gain $34 if the target hits), and writes
the plan with its invalidation: a 4H close below 140 kills the idea.
If the confirmation never comes, there is no trade — the checklist
failing is the plan working. Full walkthrough in
`reference/example-analysis-sol.md`.
